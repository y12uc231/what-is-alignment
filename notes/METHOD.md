# Method

This note describes how the corpus was assembled, how sources were read, how cases were classified, how the term-usage measurements were made, and what we could not read. The literature cutoff is November 17, 2026. We may have missed relevant work, and we welcome authors and readers to point us to it through an issue in this repository.

## Corpus

The corpus (`data/papers.jsonl`) contains arXiv papers, peer-reviewed articles outside arXiv, lab publications (blog posts, system cards, risk reports, specifications), essays on the Alignment Forum and LessWrong, company statements about incidents, and, in two cases, news reports used only for facts that the primary statements do not give. We built it in four passes. First, we listed the works that introduced or redefined the term and its close relatives, from Wiener (1960) through the 2026 lab specifications. Second, we listed the documented cases of model behavior that the literature describes as alignment failures, or that appear under the alignment heading, with a preference for primary reports that contain transcripts, rates or numbers. Third, we searched for work whose main subject is the meaning, scope or misuse of the word "alignment", and for documented incidents and definitional work from June to November 2026. Fourth, we followed the references of the central works in both directions. Every arXiv identifier was checked against arXiv's metadata (`scripts/verify_corpus.py`), and every web source records its URL and, where the page gives none, how its date was determined.

## Reading

Every work that a document in `docs/` relies on for a claim was read in full, except for the long works listed below, and its read is stored in `reads/<id>.json` with verbatim quotations that cover its method, results and limitations (`scripts/verify_reads.py` checks that every quotation occurs in the full text and that quotations come from at least three sections and from the last 40% of the body). The full texts themselves are not redistributed. Several long works were read in full in the sections that bear on definitions and cases and more selectively elsewhere: Carlsmith's "Scheming AIs" (the introduction, the author's section-by-section summary, Sections 1.1–1.3.5 and parts of Sections 5 and 6), Carlsmith's 2022 report on power-seeking (Sections 1, 2.1.2, 4.1–4.4, 5.3.4–5.4, 6.2, 7 and 8), the Ji et al. and Shen et al. surveys and Google DeepMind's 2025 safety approach (definitions, taxonomies, examples, limitations and conclusions in full; technique sections by their openings), "Truthful AI" (the executive summary, Sections 1 and 5, and Appendix A.4–A.5 in full; the governance and evaluation sections more briefly), and the ethics whitepaper of 2024 (its introduction, overview, Section 6 on model development and alignment, Section 8.3 and conclusion; the other sections were searched for the word only). The corpus entries of these works are marked `note_basis: "sections"`, and the reads of the two surveys, Google DeepMind's paper and the whitepaper record which sections were read in full.

From each source we extracted two kinds of structured records. A *definition card* (`data/definitions/`) records a definition or characterization of alignment or a closely tied term, verbatim, with its target (intentions, instructions, preferences, values and so on), its principal, whether it defines alignment by behavior or by motivation, and every explicit inclusion and exclusion the source states. A *case card* (`data/cases/`) records a documented behavior: the system, the setting (deployed, laboratory-elicited, instructed, model organism, simulated agentic scenario, training run or toy environment), what happened, verbatim quotations, the term the source uses, and the evidence on the four questions of [Section 1](../docs/01-first-principles.md). Every quotation in every card is checked against the full text by `scripts/verify_cards.py`, and every quotation of five or more words in the documents is checked by `scripts/check_doc_quotes.py`.

## Classification

Each case card carries a candidate verdict recorded at reading time and a final verdict assigned when the catalogues were written (`data/verdicts/`), under the rules of [Section 1](../docs/01-first-principles.md). The rules were refined after a first pass over the cards showed four recurring difficulties: verdicts depend on which principal is the reference, the capability question must allow for honest reporting of failure, knowledge is sufficient but not necessary evidence, and a misaligned objective must be distinguished from a misaligned system. The final verdicts apply the refined rules. Cases that several sources describe were merged, and `docs/catalogue.md` and `data/cases_final.yaml` list every case once with all of its sources, its verdict, the principal the verdict is relative to, and a one-sentence reason. Verdicts are interpretations of the evidence in the sources and are marked as such in the documents; where a source classifies its own case, we report the source's classification in its own words alongside ours.

## Term-usage measurements

The venue measurement used the accepted-paper lists of ICLR, ICML and NeurIPS for 2025 and 2026 (statuses poster, spotlight and oral; main, position and datasets tracks; workshops excluded). Of 30,986 accepted papers, 1,102 have a title containing a word that begins with "align". We drew a simple random sample of 200 with a fixed seed (`scripts/term_usage_sample.py` reproduces it; the sample and its labels are in `data/term_usage/sample200_labels.csv`) and labeled each title, reading the abstract when the title was ambiguous, with the five-sense codebook given in [Section 8](../docs/08-how-the-word-drifted.md); we labeled separately all 47 titles containing "misalign" or "unalign" but no word beginning with "align". The intervals are Wilson score intervals. A single labeler applied the codebook, and titles are a coarse signal of a paper's sense. A keyword classifier over titles and abstracts, which we tried first, misassigned many technical-matching papers to the goal sense because their abstracts use "misaligned" in the technical sense, so we report only the hand labels. The arXiv measurement counted titles containing fixed phrases in cs.CL, cs.LG, cs.AI and cs.CV per calendar year of submission from 2019 to November 17, 2026, using the arXiv API; phrase counts are lower bounds on each sense, and the "misalign" series mixes senses. `scripts/term_usage_venues.py` applies the venue population filter (and contains the keyword classifier we set aside), and `scripts/arxiv_title_trend.sh` produces the arXiv counts; the population list, the sample, the labels and the counts are in `data/term_usage/`.

## What we could not read or had to read indirectly

Some primary pages blocked automated retrieval or had changed. OpenAI's 2022 alignment-research post and 2023 superalignment post were read from 2023 web-archive captures, with the superalignment footnotes taken from the current page. Christiano's 2018 post was read from its Alignment Forum crosspost because the original site refused the request. Microsoft's February 2023 post about Bing chat was read from a web-archive capture because the original URL no longer resolves. xAI's statements about the Grok incidents of May and July 2025 were read from the posts themselves through a text-extraction proxy; news reports were used only for the content of the outputs and for one February 2025 episode that we found described only in a news report. Wiener's 1960 article was read from a scan whose extracted text has broken word spacing on the first page, so we quote only later pages; we note in [Section 2](../docs/02-origins-and-definitions.md) that a commonly reproduced shortened version of his best-known sentence omits two clauses. The specification-gaming examples list is a living document, retrieved on November 17, 2026 with 90 entries. The text of the Claude Opus 4 and Sonnet 4 system card extracted from its PDF without line structure, and some extracted papers lost mathematical symbols or hyphens, which limited which sentences could be quoted verbatim. Three documents related to the 2026 incidents were not read: the UK AI Security Institute's separate technical report on its incident, the independent review of the Hugging Face incident, and one later system card whose examples we know only through Anthropic's August 2026 risk report.
